Rollercoaster.dev — Copy Styleguide (Marketing & Landing v1)

Last updated: 2025-08-26

0. Scope & goals

Applies to: landing page, marketing site, social posts, newsletters, announcements, and product marketing surfaces (empty states, changelog intros).

Goals: Clear • credible • inclusive • low-friction for neurodivergent audiences and open‑source contributors. Reduce cognitive load; keep status transparent; avoid hype.

⸻

1. Audiences & messaging priorities

Write to tasks and outcomes.

Primary audiences
• Developers & contributors — grasp scope, see progress and issues, know where to help.
• Learners & communities — earn/keep/share credentials with evidence they control.
• Educators, mentors, orgs/meetups — no lock‑in, path to standards support (OB3/VC), honest pilot timelines.

Above‑the‑fold rule: communicate who it’s for + what it is + why it matters with one primary CTA.

⸻

2. Positioning (internal guardrail)

An open‑source toolkit to issue and keep digital credentials, built to support the 1EdTech Open Badges standard.

Use to align; don’t paste verbatim.

⸻

3. Voice & tone
   • Voice: calm, human, technically credible, plain‑spoken, never patronizing.
   • Tone by context:
   • Hero/marketing: friendly + factual (no hype).
   • Proof/roadmap: transparent, precise (dates, status).
   • Errors/status banners: empathetic, action‑oriented (what happened → what to do).
   • Bias‑free language: avoid ableist terms and stereotypes.

⸻

4. Standards, naming, and legal‑safe wording
   • Open Badges is a 1EdTech open standard. Treat as a proper noun; do not translate the name. In German, first mention may add: Open Badges (ein offener Standard für digitale Abzeichen).
   • Claims before certification (safe):
   • “Built to support the 1EdTech Open Badges standard.”
   • “Working toward Open Badges 3.0 compatibility.”
   • Avoid implying endorsement; don’t use 1EdTech logos/seals without approval.

⸻

5. Plain language & readability
   • Sentences ≈ 15–20 words; one idea per sentence.
   • Active voice; address the reader as you.
   • Define acronyms once; use descriptive links.
   • Provide TL;DR when content is complex.
   • Chunk with clear headings and short lists.

⸻

6. Neurodivergent‑friendly & accessibility rules
   • Reduce cognitive load: predictable layout, scannable headings, concise steps, preview time/effort (e.g., “~5 minutes”).
   • Prefer literal over idiomatic language; avoid metaphors that can be misread.
   • Error copy pattern: What happened → Why (if helpful) → What to do next → Link to help.

⸻

7. Landing page IA (structure only; no final copy)

Hero (above the fold)
• H1 (≤7 words, concrete)
• Short deck (1–2 sentences: what + who)
• One primary CTA; optional secondary CTA
• Optional transparency note (e.g., progress tracked on GitHub)

Below the fold 1. Who it’s for — audiences + benefits (bullets) 2. Benefits — 3–5 bullets; pair each benefit with a “how” proof 3. How it works — 3 steps with verbs + outcomes 4. Transparency — roadmap snapshot, last updated, links to issues 5. Get involved — start here (issues, docs) 6. Footer notes — standards disclaimer, licenses, privacy, accessibility

⸻

8. Microcopy patterns (templates)

Buttons/CTAs
Pattern: {Verb} + {object}
Length: 1–3 words; one primary CTA per screen.

Forms
Labels are nouns.
Help text answers “what goes here?”.
Placeholders are examples, not instructions.
Errors follow the 4‑part pattern above.

Empty states
Pattern: {What this is} + {How to start} + {Effort hint}.

Headings
Sentence case; literal; no puns.

Links
Descriptive (e.g., “Read evidence tips”), not “click here”.

Status banners
Prefix with state (“Offline: …”, “Beta: …”); end with next action.

## Humor & Playfulness

> We’re friendly and human—but clarity always wins. Use light playfulness where it helps people feel welcomed, never where it risks confusion or stress.

### Principles

- **Clarity before cleverness.** If a joke competes with comprehension, the joke loses. (Mailchimp: clarity over entertainment; Microsoft: simple and human.)
- **Delight sits on top of usability.** Add playfulness only after the core flow is strong. (NN/g & general UX guidance.)
- **Gently human, not goofy.** Warm, conversational, and brief; avoid sarcasm and inside jokes.

### Where humor belongs vs. never belongs

- **Play zones (OK to be light):** marketing/landing sections, success moments, non‑critical empty states, helper tips, and non‑blocking loading text.
- **No‑joke zones (keep it straight):** errors, permissions, payments, privacy/legal, security, account recovery, anything high‑stakes. (NN/g error‑message guidance.)

### Tone ladder (pick the lightest level that still stays clear)

- **Neutral helpful** → default everywhere.
- **Light & friendly** → marketing, success, onboarding, empty states.
- **Straight & precise** → errors, legal, payments, security.

### Humor guardrails (checklist for writers)

1. **Scan test:** remove the playful clause—does the meaning and task still hold? If not, cut it.
2. **No ambiguity:** users can still predict the action/result of CTAs and steps.
3. **Inclusive & literal:** avoid idioms/wordplay; keep bias‑free; prefer plain language that localizes cleanly.
4. **De‑stress, don’t distress:** never add humor where users are stuck or anxious (especially errors and payments).
5. **Keep it short:** microcopy stays concise and scannable; never bury instructions inside a joke.

### Microcopy patterns (templates only; do not ship verbatim)

- **Success/confirmation:** `{Result}. {Next step}. {Optional tiny aside}.`
- **Empty state:** `{What this is} + {How to start} + {Effort hint} + {Soft encouragement}.`
- **Loading (non‑critical):** `Loading {object} (~{x} seconds). {Optional light aside}.`
- **Helper text:** `{Instruction in plain language}. {Optional friendly nudge}.`
- **Errors (no humor):** `What happened → Why (if useful) → What to do next → Help link`.

### DE localization rules for playful text

- Use **du**; keep it literal and warm (no puns).
- Mirror EN tone level (neutral/helpful ↔ light/friendly ↔ straight/precise).
- **Never** add humor in DE where EN is serious (e.g., errors, legal, payments).

---

### Humor QA (add to PR template)

- **Zone check:** is this in a Play zone, not a No‑joke zone?
- **Scan test:** remove the joke—does the task remain clear and actionable?
- **Accessibility:** plain‑language + headings/lists; readable for ND users.
- **Localization:** DE can carry the wording without added complexity.
- **Legal/standards:** no implied endorsements; Open Badges naming rules respected.
